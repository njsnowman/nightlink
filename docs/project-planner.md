# RF project planner

Tracks [#165](https://github.com/njsnowman/nightlink/issues/165). Operations opens RF projects. A project keeps the study area, sites, radio assumptions, terrain, coverage and candidate decisions together. The primary interface is a seven-stage map workspace; Team & advanced tools retains earlier team, channel, checklist, field-record and individual RF tools.

## Start to finish

1. **Project.** Create a named project and describe the intended service. Set a study center/radius (1640.4–82021.0 ft) and coverage goal. You can choose the center on the map, then confirm it. The goal refers to modeled area-grid samples, not measured service.
2. **Sites.** Add coordinates, plot on the map, or copy a selected known mesh location. Mark existing transmitters (up to three), service targets and proposed candidates. A service target is a place you want served. Site locations retain their source/time. Roles are explicitly selected; a proposed repeater is not silently treated as an existing transmitter.
3. **Radio.** Confirm common frequency, power, gains/losses, sensitivity, mobile receiver height and margin. Defaults can be applied to selected sites. Existing profiles are unchecked until you explicitly choose to replace them. Review each site's actual mast height/equipment separately. Propagation k and required Fresnel fraction remain editable advanced assumptions.
4. **Terrain & LOS.** Select two to eight sites and calculate every unique pair (maximum 28 per job). Coordinates are retrieved through the shared terrain cache/service, and results save atomically. Select a path to inspect its terrain profile, ray/Fresnel clearance and reciprocal diffraction-aware reserve. Positive modeled budget does not prove LOS or field delivery. Up to 50 paths/scenarios are retained per project; unrelated saved height scenarios are preserved.
5. **Coverage.** Generate the selected existing-transmitter estimates using the same area, frequency, receiver and loss assumptions. Each origin retains its per-site equipment. The map combines the best modeled reserve at area-grid samples, with separate current/missing status per transmitter and results for selected service targets. Preview/balanced/detailed radial inputs expose their spacing and source resolution. No new calculation starts merely by opening a stage.
6. **Repeater gaps.** Only evaluated below-budget area cells become gap clusters; missing data is unknown. Proposals prioritize clusters near selected service targets, then cluster size, and choose a cell near a cluster's center. Save a proposal as a study candidate and confirm mast height. Evaluation generates its coverage plus terrain-backed paths to the existing transmitters. Compare newly covered samples, additional selected targets and best reciprocal backhaul reserve separately. Candidates with a missing or below-budget backhaul are not offered promotion. A modeled candidate can be added to deployment; it then becomes part of the selected-transmitter baseline. There is no automatic radio reconfiguration.
7. **Deployment.** Review unresolved checks, mark the plan reviewed, export the project JSON, coverage GeoJSON/CSV, project report and site installation packets. The project report includes a schematic coverage grid, source/model context and candidate summary; installation packets include site/equipment/profile details and optional team/checklists. Add field measurements, access and structural/installation information before treating a proposal as an installation decision.

All edits save locally through the existing revision-protected planner store. The last workflow stage is a local presentation preference. Existing plans keep their team, notes, paths, observations and photos. Legacy common RF defaults derive from the first existing path/profile where present; study area and transmitter roles remain explicit.

## What the coverage and gap results mean

Terrain uses Copernicus GLO-90 via Open-Meteo, documented 295.3 ft digital-surface resolution, EGM2008 orthometric heights and provider-documented 2021 release. Source, datum and fetch time are retained. A digital surface may include trees/structures; it is not surveyed bare ground. Manual ground overrides must declare the same datum; Nightlink does not transform vertical datums.

The model is Bullington ITU-R P.526 §4.5.1 plus free-space attenuation, with the entered effective-Earth k. It is **not ITM/Longley–Rice**, full delta-Bullington or verified coverage. The map resamples radial outer-center predictions to a 17×17 local study lattice, retaining cells whose centers are inside the study circle. Each cell uses the nearest bearing and containing radial range. Filled cells interpolate that prediction; they do not independently model every point of the polygon. Green is at least 10 dB reciprocal reserve, yellow 0–10 dB, red below the entered requirement and grey unknown. Reserve includes sensitivity and the required margin. Area percentages are approximate shares of equally spaced local grid samples; they are not a measured population or service percentage.

A cell is a modeled gap only when every selected transmitter has a usable prediction there and none meets the budget. Any positive prediction establishes modeled coverage at that sample; an unresolved alternative does not erase it. Candidate gain requires all selected baseline models to be current and the area grid to be resolved. Unknown service targets are not credited as a modeled improvement.

Gap clustering uses four-way lattice neighbors and the nearest actual gap cell to each cluster's centroid. Suggested coordinates are **study locations**, not identified available towers, safe/legal mast sites or terrain-optimized peaks. Candidate evaluation adds terrain and backhaul evidence; it does not independently validate land access, structures, antenna patterns, clutter, foliage, fading or weather. Compare practical sites and field measurements before deployment. No application result is automatically promoted into measured RF evidence.

Saved coverage records include the study-area snapshot, site origin, transmitter/receiver equipment and propagation settings. Changing area, frequency, origin or applicable equipment/receiver assumptions invalidates the affected model. Regenerate it; stale records remain in the backup/advanced tools and do not create gap or candidate results. Changing a common default does not silently overwrite entered per-site equipment.

## Downloads, cancellation and offline use

The same bounded terrain service is used for profiles and coverage. Explicit calculations send requested coordinates to the fixed Open-Meteo endpoint; project notes, contacts and keys are not transmitted. The public non-commercial service terms and coordinate-weighted limits apply. Settings below the map expose cache-only mode and preview detail. Request counts can be substantial: 193, 769 or 1,729 positions per transmitter/candidate, plus path samples. Shared quotas may wait or reject a job; other users of the same public IP can also affect availability.

Cancel leaves the saved project unchanged, while completed terrain batches remain cached. Provider failure, missing samples, storage failure and revision conflicts do not save partial model results. Navigating away cancels an active job. A complete model can be regenerated from cached points without elevation HTTP. Street tiles require internet or a downloaded map pack; model overlays remain available on a blank base map with a compact status notice. Navigation/interface changes may reset the map view while recalculating a stage; data stays saved.

Limits: 100 sites, three selected existing transmitters, eight stored candidates, 12 saved coverage estimates, 50 paths/scenarios and existing planner storage limits. Each automatic path is 3.3 ft–656168.0 ft; the required radius of a transmitter model is capped at 164042.0 ft. The project study radius is smaller so normal in-area origins can include the entire study region. Frequency calculations are bounded to 30–6,000 MHz. These are input/computation limits, not hardware capabilities or operating authorization.

## Compatibility and evidence

Planner IndexedDB upgrades earlier versions to version 5 while retaining records; older builds cannot write the newer planner database. Backup format 5 retains project fields, survey observations, area keys and full terrain grids; imports 1–5 are accepted. Export before upgrading if an older build must remain usable. Planner/exports are unencrypted local data. JSON is the full backup; GeoJSON, CSV and reports are derived outputs.

Automated geometry/coverage/measurement fixtures are synthetic. Native retrieval proves only that provider data can be fetched and saved, not terrain accuracy or physical radio performance. Candidate-specific validation and artifact/source checksums are recorded in the release record. Queen City Con is unchanged.

Legacy paths below the project model's 30 MHz floor remain intact in advanced RF tools. Their imported project frequency defaults to 915 MHz for review rather than rejecting the stored plan. No legacy path frequency is rewritten.

## Guided studies (rc.31, issue #167)

The right-hand panel starts with numbered **Do this / How / Then** instructions, with links to missing prerequisites. The seven stages remain freely navigable; instructions do not silently change roles or equipment. Optional studies are expandable. On compact displays the same panel stacks below the map.

**Selective calculations.** Exact-endpoint sourced terrain is reused across radio-assumption changes. Reversing a path reverses its sample order and distances. Moved endpoints invalidate terrain (0.3 ft tolerance). Existing scenarios remain stored. Current transmitter coverage with at least the requested radial detail is reused; affected area, equipment, location or radio changes rebuild only invalid models. Map evaluations use a bounded 24-record in-memory cache, keyed by the full coverage record after current-input checks. Force rebuild refreshes provider terrain when online; in cache-only mode it rebuilds from cached coordinates. A status line reports reused and rebuilt records. Reusing manually imported terrain retains its actual source and sampling rather than silently fetching another provider.

**Before / after.** In Coverage or Repeater gaps, select a candidate in the comparison section. Before shows existing transmitters; After adds the candidate; Newly covered displays only previously below-budget samples that become covered. Numeric gain requires complete current baseline and candidate models. Review reciprocal backhaul separately in candidate evaluation. Unknown data never counts as a confirmed gap or coverage gain.

**Outage simulation.** Select unavailable existing transmitters, then simulate. The map recomputes the union of remaining transmitter models and reports lost covered samples and selected service targets. Removing all transmitters makes known locations simulated gaps while retaining unknown locations as unknown. This does not model routing, repeater chains, congestion or downstream backhaul dependency. Restore baseline ends the preview. No radio command or saved-role change occurs.

**Nearby terrain search.** Select a modeled gap and search 100–2,0 ft around it. Nine DEM positions (center plus eight bearings) are retrieved. The center and up to three highest positions are screened against up to three existing transmitters and three representative gap/target locations. Each of at most 24 paths uses at most 65 samples. Ranking prioritizes usable reciprocal backhaul, representative above-budget benefit, resolved samples, backhaul reserve and elevation. Receive benefit uses the mobile reference receiver; backhaul uses actual transmitter profiles. A geometric mast suggestion attempts Fresnel clearance at the screened location up to 262.5 ft AGL; it is neither a structural recommendation nor proof of usable RF budget. Elevated points may rank poorly if backhaul or benefit is inadequate. Source, datum, sampling and retrieval time are shown. Save a point as a candidate, then run full area/backhaul evaluation before promotion. This is a sampled local search, not an exhaustive optimum, verified tower inventory or access assessment.

**Field calibration.** In Deployment select a current terrain path and record a direct endpoint field observation. Explicitly confirm that frequency and equipment match, and enter measured RSSI. Only numeric observations with a current matching model/input fingerprint enter the comparison. Unmatched, stale and no-RSSI records remain retained but are excluded. Mean error is measured RSSI minus modeled receive level; mean absolute and RMS errors are also shown. Three or more current matched observations enable an explicit conservative preview of additional loss equal to negative mean error (zero for positive bias, capped at 60 dB). This subtracts reserve from the sampled baseline; incomplete negative results remain unknown. It is a what-if allowance, not a refitted propagation model or area-wide accuracy validation. Saved radio assumptions are unchanged.

Comparison modes, outage selections, terrain-screening results and calibration previews are session-only. Saving a screened candidate retains its location, screening/source note and entered mast assumption; normal JSON backups then retain that candidate. Full candidate evaluation retains terrain/coverage through the existing model records. Deployment reports continue to export the saved baseline and candidate assessments, not an active transient outage or calibration map.

## Feet presentation and compatibility (rc.32, issue #171)

All app-owned linear measurements in the project workflow and advanced RF tools use feet: editable heights/ground elevations, study/coverage/search radii, path lengths, clearance, sample/source spacing, charts, scales and reports. Gap areas use square feet. Frequency, power, gain, reserve, coordinates and angles retain their RF/geographic units. The offline Radio line-of-sight calculator, hop-distance annotations, ANSI map scales and propagation distance column also use feet. External embedded feeds retain their provider-controlled presentation.

Numeric fields convert feet to canonical SI at save time; existing schema-4 plans and JSON backups retain their original typed SI fields for compatibility. Unchanged rounded form values preserve the original canonical number exactly. Fields display up to three decimals; RF forms convert their bounds as well as their values, with boundary tolerance limited to half the displayed precision. Feet conversion does not rewrite stored measurements or automatically change RF settings; Field Survey adds the database-version protection described below. User-entered notes and source/datum strings are retained verbatim.

The blank terrain CSV uses distance_ft,elevation_ft,obstruction_ft; importing those headers converts numeric values to canonical SI before the normal terrain validation. Existing SI CSV headers remain accepted. GeoJSON derived length properties use radiusFt, heightFt, receiverHeightFt and resolutionFt, with lengthUnit set to ft. JSON project backups retain their existing canonical schema; they are not presentation exports. Printed project/RF/site reports and terrain plot tick values use feet.

## Field Survey (rc.33, issue #173)

Open **Field survey** in the project heading. The normal seven-stage plan remains available through Return to planning or the numbered workflow buttons. The survey uses the same map with three right-panel steps:

1. **Choose location.** Select a saved link and direction, a selected service target with a transmitter, or another point. Choose actual coordinates manually, on the map, or through Use device / GPS location. Selecting a planned location does not assert that a test occurred there. The explicit location action uses the existing Windows location provider in the desktop app or browser geolocation in preview. It may be coarse, denied or unavailable; it is not guaranteed GNSS and does not use an app IP-location fallback. Source, captured time and stated accuracy are shown. Opening Survey does not request device location or elevation data. The existing basemap may request visible street tiles when online and retains its offline fallback.
2. **Record test.** Perform a direct endpoint test using the radios, then record the receiving endpoint's outcome, RSSI/SNR if measured, actual coordinates, receiving antenna height in feet, frequency, time, call sign and conditions. Confirm actual coordinates and a matching direct setup. The form does not connect, configure, transmit or read statistics automatically. A normal forwarded packet's RSSI is not an endpoint test. Unconfirmed records can be saved but do not enter numeric comparison. Draft form text is retained locally; revision conflicts leave it available for recovery.
3. **Review evidence.** Observed points show reported heard, intermittent or not-heard outcomes; grey means unconfirmed location. The baseline heatmap remains a model and can be hidden. Individual observed points never interpolate a measured coverage surface. Review follow-up priorities, inspect a record, center its position, plan a retest, or export CSV/GeoJSON/HTML and a full project backup. Coordinate accuracy is not measurement confidence or RF reliability.

Each of up to 500 observations stores an immutable model snapshot when available. Direct-path snapshots predict the chosen direction with the actual entered receiving height and saved transmitter/receiver equipment. Position plus stated accuracy must fall within 328.1 ft of the receiving endpoint for that comparison. Target/free-point snapshots sample the selected transmitter's current radial coverage only when frequency and reference-receiver height match. These radial comparisons inherit the sampled model's interpolation limits. Wrong frequency, missing/stale terrain or absent coverage produces an unmodeled observation, not a fake zero or gap. Unconfirmed positions/setup or stated location accuracy worse than 328.1 ft are excluded from numeric review.

Comparisons distinguish current, stale, unconfirmed and unmodeled records. Measured minus modeled RSSI and measured reserve are calculated only for current confirmed setups with numeric RSSI. Missing RSSI stays absent. Changing equipment, terrain, path geometry or relevant coverage inputs leaves historical snapshots intact and marks the affected comparisons stale. Follow-up prioritization uses the latest observation per link/direction or target/transmitter: modeled-above-budget but not heard, intermittent reception, other reported failures, below-margin readings, untested targets and stale/unconfirmed data. These are investigation tasks, not automatic repeater-placement recommendations or deployment acceptance.

Current direct-path survey RSSI from matching common-project assumptions can join the conservative calibration preview in Deployment. Radial observations and saved height-scenario paths do not enter that preview. Legacy RF observations remain separate records; Survey hides their markers to avoid confusing them with the current survey collection. Survey records remain visible as history when a related site/path is removed. Duplicating a project resets survey execution records along with other execution state; the original retains them.

Planner database and backup format **6** retain survey records and prevent older writers from opening the upgraded database. Backup imports accept formats 1–6. Export a backup before upgrading if retaining a rollback copy is necessary; older versions do not read format 6. JSON backups retain canonical model units. Survey CSV/GeoJSON present height/accuracy in feet, actual coordinates, measured values, comparison state and recorded prediction; CSV text fields are protected against spreadsheet formula interpretation. Printable project/site reports include the survey observations and follow-up list. No physical-radio, actual device-location or installation-validation result is inferred from software fixtures.


## Network Engineering Studio (rc.34)

Open Engineering studio from a project. The map remains beside five guided stages: Set objectives, Compare designs, Stress-test, Validate and Deploy. Engineering and Field Survey are separate modes; observations remain available. Select up to eight study sites and up to three explicit gateways/service anchors. Gateway status is an entered service dependency, not an inferred internet connection.

Existing selected transmitters and required gateways remain in each design. Optional new sites can use their current setup, up to two additional mast heights and two additional equipment choices, with a maximum of three new sites. Enter site capital estimates, incremental mast cost per foot, mast limits and equipment price differences. Existing transmitter capital is treated as sunk (zero incremental). Missing estimates stay unknown. Search uses a beam of 48 and at most 5,000 expanded designs; it is not a global-optimum guarantee. Cost and budget conclusions require complete entered estimates.

Connected service requires a modeled reciprocal RF path to an explicit gateway. Unknown paths differ from known isolated nodes. Coverage counts sampled study positions and selected service targets, not people, traffic capacity or delivery probability. The search can prioritize connected coverage, lowest entered cost meeting the goal, or worst single-site-failure service. Failure studies remove each site (including gateways), known modeled links and entered shared power groups. A lone gateway is a single failure point. Actual protocol routing, interference, airtime and congestion are not simulated.

Validate opens sourced sampled 3D terrain, path rays, the selected Fresnel boundary and entered sampled obstructions. Area geometry requires compatible terrain datum; otherwise a labeled path corridor is shown. Unknown clutter and terrain outside the supplied samples remain unknown. Camera angle and vertical exaggeration never change RF calculations. Obstruction entries apply only to their nearest supplied path sample. Sensitivity explicitly recalculates eight entered-design/mast/loss/sensitivity cases; invalid assumptions remain labeled.

Deploy saves up to 24 immutable scenarios, compares versions and identifies stale source inputs. Recalculation retains the saved chosen mast, equipment, costs and power assumptions while applying current source coordinates, ground, radio and available terrain. It does not replace a historical saved scenario; save a new version explicitly. Installation packets contain selected equipment/heights, proposed reciprocal links, sourced terrain/profile plots, true bearings, power arithmetic, entered gear and field checklists. Load/solar/battery numbers are assumptions: autonomy is usable Wh / average W; daily balance is harvest less load, without weather/aging unless entered.

### Optional Run full study

Use Run full study at any engineering stage. One form collects study center/radius, objective/goal/new-site cap, optional budget, mast choices, common mobile RF assumptions, selected site coordinates/equipment and explicit gateways. Optional power/cost inputs expand with each site's details. Existing project targets, equipment alternatives, gear and notes are retained. Add new sites in the project first. All human length inputs/plots/reports use feet; JSON backups keep canonical model units.

Run saves the inputs, then prepares every selected site pair's terrain/LOS, selected-site coverage, bounded design comparison, all selected-design failure cases, eight sensitivity cases per selected site and a combined HTML report with coverage plot, 3D snapshot when available, source provenance, installation packets and field checks. A design scenario is saved by default; the report downloads by default and can be downloaded again during the session. Back up the project to retain models and scenarios. The report itself is a separate downloaded file and is not embedded in the database.

Only an explicit run retrieves missing terrain. Current geometry/models are reused unless rebuilding is selected. Cached-only makes no elevation HTTP requests; missing coordinates in the cache stop the job with an error. Online requests send sample coordinates to Open-Meteo under its documented provider limits; the shared service displays progress/waits. Cancel or provider failure retains saved inputs and completed cached points, but saves no partial study paths/coverage/scenario. A revision conflict prevents final model writes. No radio activity is issued by the study.

A completed report can still show REVIEW REQUIRED: unmet service/budget goals, unknown paths/costs, unavailable 3D or invalid sensitivity cases do not become successful deployment findings. Field measurements remain separate from model estimates. No hardware, actual-site or installation acceptance is implied.

Database and backup format 6 retain engineering scenarios; imports accept 1–6. Older database writers cannot open the upgraded store. Retain a pre-upgrade backup for rollback; older releases cannot read format 6. Project duplication remaps planning inputs and resets scenarios/execution history; site removal keeps historical scenarios.


## Verify & refine (rc.35)

Open Verify & refine from the project heading. It uses the existing map and five ordered stages over the saved source plan; transient Engineering designs are not applied automatically. Queen City Con is unchanged.

1. Verification queue prioritizes current unexpected outcomes and low margins, stale/unconfirmed observations, unresolved links, untested directions/targets, and representative baseline gap/unknown samples. It includes up to 300 displayed tasks, with a truncation notice; readiness uses the entire bounded derived queue. The order is an investigation priority, not a travel route or site-access assessment. Center a planned test or open it in Field Survey. No observation is created until the operator records it; actual location/setup confirmation is still required. New healthy matching evidence resolves its setup's task without deleting history.
2. Evidence map shows the latest observation for each link direction, target/transmitter or point/transmitter. Hollow rings show modeled above/below-budget states; filled centers show reported heard/intermittent/not-heard outcomes. Grey rings mean unresolved comparisons. Outcome or at least 10 dB RSSI differences are investigation signals, not confidence scores. Point observations never interpolate measured area-wide coverage. Older evidence remains in Field Survey.
3. Explain improvements explicitly evaluates +10 ft at either endpoint of weak source-plan links (less than 6 dB reciprocal modeled reserve), respecting entered mast limits. Results show calculated reserve/clearance change, entered incremental mast-only cost or unknown cost, and limitations. Zero/negative benefit remains visible. Missing terrain remains unresolved. No source edits, terrain retrieval or radio commands occur; evaluate wider equipment/network alternatives in Engineering studio.
4. Deployment reviews track access/permissions, equipment/feedline, power, installation/mast and field RF review per site. Confirmed/blocked entries require a reviewer and evidence/reason. Source changes mark confirmations stale; new relevant observations additionally invalidate the RF review. History is retained. Reviews recorded means the checklist is recorded and no site-linked queued tests remain; it is not engineering, safety, regulatory or RF acceptance.
5. Project changes saves up to 12 immutable compact checkpoints. Compare source settings, terrain/coverage fingerprints and metadata, modeled baseline result, evidence states and reviews against another checkpoint or the current plan. Different grids/result bases suppress a misleading coverage delta. Listed changes are associated inputs, not isolated physical causes. Checkpoints do not contain full terrain grids and cannot restore an older project; export project JSON for complete data.

Verification reports include the queue, evidence coordinate plot/table, evaluated improvement options, readiness checks and latest-checkpoint differences when present. Full study also evaluates mast explanations and includes verification/readiness sections. Reported field outcomes remain operator-entered; modeled results do not become physical evidence.

Map scales, horizontal path distances and study/search radii now display miles. Antenna AGL, ground/elevation and vertical clearance remain feet. Canonical SI model/backup values and geographic coordinates are retained; unchanged rounded form values preserve original inputs. Terrain CSV remains feet for distance/elevation with legacy SI imports supported.

Planner database/backup format 7 retains deployment reviews/checkpoints; imports accept 1–7. Older writers are blocked after upgrade. Retain a pre-upgrade backup for rollback. Project duplication resets reviews/checkpoints and other execution state. Site removal retains review/checkpoint history; removed sites do not enter current readiness.


## Deployment workspace (rc.36)

Open Deployment workspace from the project heading. Eight deployment records per project are supported. It reuses the map with five ordered stages; source planning, deployment records and actual radio settings stay separate. No stage configures radios, transmits tests, retrieves elevation or sends work orders to other people automatically.

1. **Approve baseline.** Promote a saved engineering scenario whose source signature is current. Name the baseline approver and explicitly confirm the selection. The record freezes site coordinates, chosen equipment/heights, gateway roles, modeled summary, access notes, selected targets and compact modeled-link/source references. It contains no raw terrain grids; preserve a full project backup for the original study data. Later source edits, scenario deletion or source-site removal do not rewrite this baseline. Unmet modeled goals remain frozen review items; correct the design and promote a replacement deployment instead of dismissing them as completed installation work.
2. **Site work orders.** Assign an owner/call sign, target date/time in the project timezone, access notes, equipment quantities/specifications and installation tasks. Complete status requires an owner and all listed tasks checked. Gear starts from the selected scenario's entered equipment list. Work orders are local records; no messages or assignments are sent externally.
3. **Field acceptance.** Promotion creates bidirectional tests for modeled feasible site pairs and target tests using the strongest modeled connected transmitter when known, otherwise a declared gateway. Review these criteria before field use. Add site, selected-target or other mobile-location tests. Default criteria require reported heard reception, at least three attempts, at least 95% successful attempts and a receiving-position tolerance of 328.1 ft; RSSI/SNR thresholds are optional until entered. Site tests use actual installed receiving coordinates/AGL; mobile tests use their entered approved point/height. Record the actual time, observer, receiving position/AGL/frequency, reception, counts, measured RSSI/SNR and conditions. Confirm actual location and direct setup. Actual transmitter/receiver installations must be recorded before site tests can pass; mobile tests need the actual transmitter installation. Missing required numeric values, wrong setup/position/frequency/height or insufficient counts remain incomplete. Results retain immutable criteria/setup snapshots. Changed RF installations or criteria make affected results stale; new tests do not overwrite history. Equal observation times use append order to identify the latest result. Optional tests remain visible but do not block handover.
4. **Punch list.** Generated items include frozen design review issues, missing target positions, blocked/incomplete work, missing as-built records, entered-versus-installed equipment shortages and required untested/incomplete/failed/stale tests. Correct the underlying record to clear a generated item. Enter additional issues with priority, site and owner; resolution requires an owner and evidence notes. Resolved entered issues remain available for review. Names, dates and notes are local planning data, not verified external approvals.
5. **As-built & handover.** Record actual coordinates, equipment/serial, frequency, antenna AGL/ground datum, RF specifications/assumptions, gear and optional power figures. Approved values prefill forms for review; saving requires actual-installation confirmation, a named recorder and evidence notes. Prior installed revisions are retained (up to 100); up to 500 field results and 200 entered issues are supported. Differences show approved versus actual values without recalculating or claiming real area-wide coverage. Handover is an explicit operator record, allowed only after the generated/manual punch list is empty, with at least one required passing test. It is not structural/electrical/regulatory certification or a delivery guarantee. Twenty handover records are supported; later deployment changes mark prior handovers historical.

Export a self-contained deployment HTML report, reference JSON for the individual record, or a complete project backup. The HTML includes baseline provenance, work/tasks/gear, acceptance criteria/latest states, all field-test history, punch history, as-built differences/revisions and operator handovers. Standalone deployment JSON is a reference export; restore through the complete Operations backup. Removing a deployment uses an explicit confirmation and removes its local history; export first when it must be retained.

Database/backup format 8 accepts formats 1–8 and blocks older database writers after upgrade. Retain a pre-upgrade backup for rollback. Project duplication resets deployments and other execution records; original records remain intact. As before, map distances/scales use miles, AGL/elevations use feet and model/backup values stay canonical. Queen City Con is unchanged.


## Project Command Center (rc.37)

Opening a project starts at Command Center. The map remains central, with Plan / Deploy / Verify / Handover above it. The right panel contains the project scope, an explicit deployment selection, a prioritized next action and Overview / Selected site / Change impact tabs. Map-layer and site selectors sit above the map. Tabs and site selections retain the same map element and viewport. The source-planning layer always uses the current source baseline, including after leaving an outage or candidate what-if preview.

The first missing source input, incomplete model, deployment task or acceptance issue links to its existing editor or workflow. Plan opens the appropriate numbered planning step; the original seven-step workflow and advanced tools remain available. With several deployments, choose one explicitly; a sole deployment is initially selected. A removed saved selection returns to source-only context instead of silently switching to another record. This per-project selection is a local interface preference, not part of exported project data.

Selected site compares current source coordinates / RF assumptions, frozen approved variant and recorded installation. It includes associated work, source observations, deployment point tests, punch items, reviews and checkpoint changes. A removed source site can still be inspected through its frozen or installed record. Source point observations made at a different current study frequency or historical height scenario are identified separately; they remain preserved, with their original exact-setup comparison. Neither these points nor deployment acceptance outcomes establish area-wide coverage.

Change impact lists missing/stale paths and coverage, stale source records/scenarios/reviews, affected deployment tests and latest-checkpoint differences. Source-model changes and installed-configuration changes are separate: changing the source does not rewrite a deployment baseline, installation, test or handover. Recalculate affected source models is explicit. It reuses matching path terrain and matching raw radial elevation surfaces, recalculating only missing or affected source models. Saved terrain reuse requires matching position, study radius, sampling and compatible ground datum; missing terrain requires the configured provider or a cached offline response. Sampling detail can require a rebuild. Cancellation, retrieval errors and save conflicts leave stored models unchanged; the complete result saves with revision checking. Recalculation never renews observations, approved scenarios or handovers.

Export Command Center report includes scope, selected deployment, work/test states, source / approved / installed height comparison and impact/checkpoint summaries. Back up complete project retains all source and execution data. Database / backup format remains 8, with no new migration. Maps and horizontal distances use miles; AGL and elevations use feet. All records are local; opening the center does not configure a radio, transmit tests or contact a terrain service. Queen City Con remains unchanged.


## Guided Operations (rc.38)

Opening a project now starts at its first unfinished source-planning step. The seven stages are Project, Sites, Radio, Terrain & LOS, Coverage, Repeater gaps and Review & export. The right panel shows required saved items, a single Continue action and its lock reason. Detailed instructions are under How to complete this step. Ready means required source work is saved; it is not physical-site approval. You may revisit earlier unlocked steps. Changing source inputs relocks dependent stages automatically, while retaining old models and evidence.

The gates require: a saved study area; at least one existing transmitter and coordinates for all selected site roles; RF profiles for selected sites; sampled current inter-transmitter terrain paths when there is more than one existing transmitter; complete current existing-transmitter coverage; and current coverage/sampled backhaul for each selected candidate. An obstructed path or below-budget result is assessed work, and does not need to be made positive to unlock review. One transmitter has no inter-transmitter path requirement. Direct service-target/custom paths and adding a repeater candidate are optional. A missed modeled coverage goal remains visible for review; it does not pretend a successful RF design or force invented evidence.

Planning Next controls and numbered navigation enforce the same saved-data gates, including direct source-step links from Command Center. Reopening a saved/imported plan derives readiness from its actual inputs/models; there are no manually checked completion flags. Errors, cancellation and save conflicts do not unlock a stage. Backward edits remain available. The Review & export stage is the source-planning endpoint; installation and field acceptance remain distinct.

Command Center remains available as a project overview. Other tools and project overview contains Engineering studio (including optional Run full study), Field survey, Verify & refine, Deployment workspace and team/advanced tools.
